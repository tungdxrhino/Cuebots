HOW TO CHOOSE THE RIGHT SHAFT

The right shaft is the one that supports your delivery, connects correctly to your cue and gives you feedback you can trust under pressure.

CUEBOTS Equipment Team · Updated July 2026

In this guideStart with your strokeDiameter and taperJoint compatibilityFeedback and deflectionShortlist checklist

Start with the stroke you already own

A shaft should make your delivery easier to repeat. Before comparing specifications, notice whether your bridge is short or long, whether you use a compact or flowing stroke and how much tactile feedback you prefer at contact.

Players with a longer bridge often appreciate a taper that stays consistent farther back. Players who use a compact bridge may notice diameter and tip response more than the length of the taper.

Choose diameter and taper together

Smaller diameters can make precise tip placement feel more visible, while larger diameters can feel familiar and stable. Neither is automatically more accurate. The useful choice is the one that keeps your bridge relaxed.

  • 11.8–12.0 mm: precise visual reference and a slimmer bridge feel.
  • 12.3–12.5 mm: a balanced middle ground for many playing styles.
  • 12.8–13.0 mm: a fuller, traditional bridge feel.
Practical testMake ten stop shots and ten long follow shots. The right diameter should disappear from your attention rather than forcing you to adjust your bridge.

Confirm joint compatibility before checkout

Joint name, pin diameter, thread and pilot shape all matter. Compare your current butt against the product listing and use the joint guide if the connection is unfamiliar. Do not force a new shaft onto a pin that does not thread smoothly by hand.

Balance feedback with deflection

Low-deflection construction can reduce the amount of aim compensation needed on side-spin shots, but feel remains personal. Some players want a crisp response; others prefer a softer, quieter hit. Use deflection as one decision factor, not the only factor.

Build a shortlist, then decide

  1. Confirm the exact joint.
  2. Select two realistic diameters.
  3. Choose a taper that suits your bridge length.
  4. Decide how crisp or muted you want contact to feel.
  5. Check shaft length, weight and tip specification.
