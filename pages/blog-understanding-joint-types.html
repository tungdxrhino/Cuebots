UNDERSTANDING CUE JOINT TYPES

A compatible joint is more than a familiar name. Pin, thread, pilot and collar must work together correctly.

CUEBOTS Equipment Team · Updated July 2026

In this guideJoint anatomyCommon familiesHow to verifySafe fitting

Know the four compatibility points

Start with pin diameter and thread pattern, then inspect whether the joint is flat-faced or piloted. Finally, compare collar diameter and material so the assembled cue feels and looks correct.

Common joint families

Popular families include radial-style pins, 3/8×10, 3/8×11, Uni-Loc quick release and 5/16 threaded joints. Similar-looking hardware can still use different threads, so visual identification is only the first step.

  • Radial-style: broad rounded threads and a firm hand-tight connection.
  • 3/8 threaded: several pitches exist; count and profile matter.
  • Quick release: short engagement designed for rapid assembly.
  • 5/16 threaded: commonly paired with piloted or flat-faced constructions.

Verify before ordering

  1. Check the cue maker and model.
  2. Photograph the pin straight on and from the side.
  3. Measure pin diameter and exposed length.
  4. Count threads over a known distance.
  5. Confirm whether the shaft insert is piloted.
Do not guessIf the joint is uncertain, send clear photographs and measurements to support before placing the shaft order.

Fit a new shaft safely

Start threading by hand with the cue held straight. The shaft should engage smoothly without tools or heavy pressure. Stop immediately if the thread binds, rocks or requires force.
